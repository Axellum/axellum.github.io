Native Home Assistant display

A wall display that runs directly on the M5Stack Tab5, with no browser: Home Assistant pushes what changed, and the screen redraws only that. It is the screen of my house.

What the display does

Everything I need day to day, on a 5 or 7 inch touch screen, without opening an app.

Weather

Rain in the next hour in 9 bars, hourly and 15-day forecasts, official weather warnings. A central card rotates every 8 seconds: work hours, rain graph, warnings, a 3-day summary.

The house

Up to 10 rooms of 20 devices (lights, switches, shutters, players, scenes, sensors) and a "House" view with "all off".

Lights and shutters

Brightness arc, 3 whites and 12 colours. Shutters open, closed or at a position; saying "Stop" stops the shutter.

Heating and cooling

Modes, thermostat arc, presets, airflow, and the temperature history over 24 hours, 7 or 30 days.

Energy

Flows between solar panels, home, grid and battery, a learned production forecast, a monthly balance in kWh (optional).

Music

Any Home Assistant player, with cover art, volume, favourite playlists and multi-room with Music Assistant.

Calendar and alarm

A monthly calendar computed on the screen (work hours, public holidays, appointments) and an alarm clock with a spoken briefing of the day.

Voice

"Okay Nabu" detected on the screen itself, with a choice of two assistants.

And more

Plants (Bluetooth sensors), Samsung TV remote, still camera images, fridges and freezers, sensor tracking, a diagnostics console, and 8 small offline games (experimental).

Supported screens

M5Stack Tab5

1280 × 720, ESP32-P4. Three display chips exist: ST7123 (mine), ST7121 (works for another user), ILI9881C (builds, untested).

The Tab5 and its three versions

Waveshare ESP32-P4 7B

1024 × 600, experimental, but it has been my everyday screen since 14 October 2026. No motion sensor, clock chip or battery; one energy page is not adapted to its screen yet.

Install

No need to compile: you need Chrome or Edge on a computer and a USB-C cable that carries data.

  1. Add the Home Assistant files, with HACS (the "Tab5" integration) or with the archive to unzip into config.
  2. Install the firmware from the install page, then the Wi-Fi.
  3. Add the screen to Home Assistant (ESPHome discovery).
  4. Pick the weather sources and calendars.
  5. Describe rooms, tiles and sensors with a blueprint, without reflashing.

Updates then come through Home Assistant; the screen only accepts firmware signed by the project. Everything is in the installation guide.

How it works

  • Home Assistant pushes, the screen never polls. The project's automations send changes to the screen, which redraws only what moved.
  • The screen only sends events, which Home Assistant turns into actions from an allow list.
  • ESPHome and LVGL 9.5, with the logic in C++; one board chosen at build time, with its own geometry.
  • Tested off the tablet: C++ tests on a PC, screen renders in continuous integration, and more than 70 written architecture decisions.
