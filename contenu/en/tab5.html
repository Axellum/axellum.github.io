M5Stack Tab5 as a Home Assistant display

The tablet the project was born for: a 5-inch 1280 × 720 touch screen built around an ESP32-P4. M5Stack has made it with three different display chips; the firmware exists for all three.

The three versions of the Tab5

M5Stack has changed the chip driving the screen and touch several times. Each needs its own firmware: they are not interchangeable. The install page asks you to pick yours.

ST7123

Made from 14 October 2025 to 28 April 2026. Display and touch in a single chip.

Supported: the version the project was developed and checked on, until my tablet failed on 11 October 2026. It is the default; the 3.9.0 firmware has not run on a device yet.

ST7121

Made since 28 April 2026. Display and touch in a single chip.

Runs for a user since October 2026 (display, touch and wake word); also installed on my second tablet on 21 October 2026.

ILI9881C

The original version, made from 9 May to 14 October 2025, with a separate GT911 touch chip.

Builds, never tried: the firmware is built on every change, but nobody has run it on this version yet.

How to tell which one you have: the display chip is printed on the sticker on the back, just above the Espressif logo. Beware, a unit labelled "ST7123" may carry an ST7121: if unsure, try one firmware then the other.

Have an ST7121 or an ILI9881C and willing to try? Tell how it went in the hardware compatibility Discussions: that is what moves these versions forward.

The ESPHome configuration

To start from scratch with ESPHome on the Tab5: a short, complete example that brings up the display, the touch, Wi-Fi and the backlight, shows a Home Assistant temperature and turns a light on. Pick your tablet's chip at the top of the file, then replace the two entities with your own.

Validated and compiled with ESPHome 2026.9.1 on 2026-10-28, not tried as is on the tablet: its pins and settings are those of the full firmware, which runs on mine.

The settings that matter on this tablet

  • The ESP32-C6, which carries the Wi-Fi, is powered through an I/O expander (PI4IOE5V6408, pin 0 of the second one): without the wifi_power switch turned on at start-up, the SDIO link never comes up ("sdmmc_card_init failed"). Its reset, GPIO15, is active high.
  • The panel reset goes through the other expander, which needs a second after start-up: without the wait in on_boot, black screen after a software restart. It has to block, hence the C delay().
  • The panel is portrait (720 × 1280): LVGL turns it to landscape with rotation: 270, and turns the touches with it.
  • No mirroring on the display or the touch: it corrupts the picture on an ST7121.
  • The MIPI-DSI link is powered by LDO 3 of the ESP32-P4, at 2.5 V (esp_ldo).
  • Home Assistant takes its time: with no API client, ESPHome restarts after 15 minutes by default, hence reboot_timeout: 60min. For the button to act, allow the device to perform actions in its ESPHome options.

The full firmware keeps the hardware in tab5-hardware.yaml (sound, microphones, clock and battery included) and the display chip in one file per version (ecran-st7123.yaml, ecran-st7121.yaml, ecran-ili9881c.yaml). The same tablet without Home Assistant: Vigie on the Tab5.

What is inside

  • ESP32-P4: dual-core RISC-V processor running the whole firmware; 32 MB of PSRAM, essential to hold the 1280 × 720 image in memory, and 16 MB of flash.
  • A separate ESP32-C6 for Wi-Fi 6 and Bluetooth 5, linked to the P4; transparent for ESPHome.
  • Backed-up clock (RX8130CE): time and alarm are right from boot, before the network.
  • Sound and microphones: an ES8388 converter for the speaker, an ES7210 for the wake-word microphones.
  • Motion sensor, used by the games you play by tilting the tablet.
  • Optional 2S lithium battery, with charger and voltage reading; the firmware turns the charger off when there is no battery or when the tablet gets hot.

Measured power draw at the USB input: 2.6 W with the screen at 100%, 1.7 W at 50%, 1.2 W with the screen off.

Install it

  1. Connect the tablet to a computer with a USB-C cable that carries data, and open the install page in Chrome or Edge.
  2. Pick your display version (ST7123, ST7121 or ILI9881C), install, then give it the Wi-Fi.
  3. Follow the Home Assistant steps: files, adding the device, weather sources, blueprint.

What the screen does once installed: the native Home Assistant display. Another board, bigger and cheaper: the Waveshare ESP32-P4 7B.
